What is simplified
  • The picture is a 2D slice through the middle of the droplet and is not to scale. A real objective is millimetres wide and sits much further from the sample; here it is drawn as one lens just below the coverslip.
  • The objective accepts a ray by its angle (set by the NA), not by where it hits the lens. Moving the objective does not change which rays are accepted, only where they land on the camera.
  • A real objective has many lens elements and a tube lens, and the image is magnified and flipped. Here the camera image is shown at the same size as the sample. It is mirrored left to right, which a real lens also does.
  • Only ray optics is included: refraction, total internal reflection and partial reflection. There is no diffraction, so a point in focus is perfectly sharp.
  • Fluorescence assumes the dye is excited evenly everywhere. Very weak reflected rays are not drawn but are counted in the camera image.
